Desk workers are seeing our work accelerate (and our specific touch diminish) as artificial intelligence becomes ascendant. I and everyone else has been wondering where there might still be room for human excellence in ten years. Before harnesses started editing files autonomously on my system, when they were writing code and abstracts, snippets of difficult emails and bits of ephemera for me to copy and paste, I thought AI would make editors out of us, and far from leveling the playing field, poor editors would let through shoddy copy, and good editors would rise in rank. That model is still valid, but there are other managerial positions in the world. Perhaps we will need to become more than one kind of manager.

Ever since Timmy Chalamet portrayed Paul Atreides, I've been obsessed with Frank Herbert's Dune Series. I finished the last book in the series today, which included detailed accounts of large-scale battle. Herbert imagines the greatest military commander, capable of ingesting military intelligence during battle and reacting immediately with decisive force. Consider the graduate student, with more tabs open on his terminal than she knows what to do with, each marshaling several agents, the grad student unable to focus on one source of detailed information at a time. Each agent is pioneering new kinds of analysis, and the grad student must ensure that the codebase each of these sessions shares does not become scrambled, that it all coalesces into decisive victory: a publication. I'm not sure what the exact mapping for this metaphor is: a commander who is indecisive, who sends out too many expeditions, is doomed to fail? Too much exploring not enough exploiting? Or is it in the kind of mental discipline a military commander must use to pay attention to the urgency of each thing at once, always questing for the thread to pull on and exploit the gaps in the opponent's defenses. I don't know.

Choosing a military commander as a role model for an AI agent orchestrator begs the question: who is the enemy? Yourself? And what is victory?
