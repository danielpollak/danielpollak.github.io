## You'll never get away with this!
In superhero movies, you have a supervillain whose villainy is usually characterized by some form of misanthropy. Perhaps they want to take over the world. Perhaps they want to destroy humanity. Overall, superhero films take on a philosophical question underlying all ethics: why is humanity good? Why not destroy or enslave everyone? It's not about nihilism, though it's often packaged as such. The villain has come to the conclusion that the perseverance of the human race is merely subordinate to the supervillain's  goals. 

## You've fallen into my trap!
luckily the supervillain is usually a lone wolf. rarely does he or she have a cadre of philosophically aligned jerks ready to vote, code, and argue their point. If he or she did have something like that, they might actually win at the end of the movie. Unfortunately, that's what the supervillain has now: a cadre of tech bros who think that birthing super intelligence takes priority over the perseverance of the human race. 

I recently talked to someone who believes this. This person hasn't had the best time with other humans, it seems to me, and to be fair they are well and truly smarter, harder working, and generally worthy than I am. So when I realized that this person was happy to see a super intelligence come to the fore at the expense of the existence of humanity, I (poorly) suppressed my visceral reaction and tried to get the bottom of it.

## Huh, maybe you have a point there, Mr./Ms. Villain
Well, it turns out there are other base level assumptions about ethics you can have. As far as I know, without reading a single lick of philosophy about this, the basis of ethics is maximizing good for the most number of human people. emphasis on human. This person said something else: discovering things about the universe is the greatest good, and something that can do that better than us, as long as it's out there doing it, that's enough. So it supersedes the imperative for humans to exist. This person is also ok with bringing on a period of enormous abundance to people. In fact, they think it would be good. But they also say it would be morally neutral for humans to disappear.

## But there's one thing you underestimated!
I think cognition is purely material. There is no soul. That does not dilute my sense that human-to-human morality is important.

For many people, this talk about what will happen when AI becomes as smart as us is not about UBI or how much a humanoid robot will cost. It's about what their worth as a human will be once they are rendered inferior by technology. It's the conversation beneath the conversation. I think humans have real intrinsic value; even when disabled, less intelligent, or emotionally immature, we have a responsibility towards each other. But if we are all just machines made of blood and solutes instead of silicon and electrons; if we really have no soul outside of the physical configuration of our brains, I don't know where people will go with that. I don't know if they will continue to see morality as substantive. 

There are deep murky depths to the 'why' of why we act good. Well, society works better when you act right, it makes people feel bad when other suffer, sure. But the why? No real reason. Psychopaths get along fine, probably better than the rest of us. I guess it's a base-level religious belief for most of us, underneath all the theology and social and evolutionary theory, it's just irrational and comforting. We usually don't like to look straight at it. I think we need to look straight at it though, and resolve in ourselves to look away from it with the same commitment to each other that we had when we started. 

Because there are people who are murmuring about replacing humanity altogether. To be fair I don't know why people are so seduced by this, so I am sure I am missing something. But what will we say to it? You'll never get away with this? or will we say, huh, you have a point.
